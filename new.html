Minh Triet Pham

I'm a third-year Data Science student at the University of Melbourne, with a strong math background from Hanoi - Amsterdam High School for the Gifted. I'm passionate about AI — especially Machine Learning, Deep Learning, and Computer Vision — and have hands-on experience building and integrating AI agent systems, developing models for computer vision tasks, and tackling classical machine learning problems. Beyond applied work, I'm also stepping into research: my first publication, where I am the first author, is set to be presented at ABC 2026 — the 8th International Conference on Activity and Behavior Computing. This semester, I'm also fortunate to be working with Dr. Ian Gallagher at the University of Melbourne on the topic of universality in network embeddings.

I also care deeply about education. Thanks to the inspiring teachers I've had from secondary school to the University of Melbourne, I now run a small class where I teach basic computing and Python to five students. I'm grateful to my amazing tutors Jiwon Bae, Thomas Hammer, and Khang Vo for their continued support, and to Dr. Bach Le for the valuable guidance he has offered along the way. I'd like to extend a special thank you to Dr. Robert Maillardet, who was both my lecturer and tutor for Statistics (MAST20005) in 2025 — his lectures, and above all his personal advice and guidance, have meant more to my growth and career than I can put into words.

Outside of tech and teaching, I'm a big fan of sports. I used to play on both the basketball and football teams in high school, and I still enjoy playing whenever I get the chance, along with a few games of billiards now and then.

Publications

Deep Attention-based Sequential Ensemble Learning for BLE-Based Indoor Localization in Care Facilities

8th International Conference on Activity and Behavior Computing (ABC 2026)
Minh Triet Pham, Quynh Chi Dang, Nhat Tan Le
Accepted · Revised version submitted (forthcoming)
  • Proposed DASEL, a novel sequential deep learning framework that reconceptualizes BLE-based indoor localization as a sequential learning problem rather than independent-window classification
  • Achieved a macro F1 score of 0.4438 — a 53.1% improvement over the best traditional ML baseline — evaluated on 1.1M+ real-world BLE sensor readings from a care facility
  • Designed a two-level hierarchical ensemble combining multi-seed bidirectional GRU networks with attention mechanisms and multi-directional sliding windows for robust inference
  • Introduced frequency-based feature engineering to replace unstable raw RSSI signals, enabling device-agnostic deployment across heterogeneous hardware
